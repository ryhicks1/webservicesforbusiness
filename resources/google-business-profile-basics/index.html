Google Business Profile basics

The listing many customers check before they call. Here is what to get right first.

Google Business Profile (GBP) is the panel that appears for many local searches and on Maps. For service and storefront businesses, it often matters as much as the website — sometimes more. The site and the profile should agree.

Claim and verify

If you have not claimed the listing, start there. Verification proves you represent the business. Keep recovery emails and phone numbers current so you do not lose access later.

Name, categories, and description

  • Business name — use the real trading name. Do not stuff keywords into the name field.
  • Primary category — pick the closest match for what you mainly sell. Secondary categories can help, but one clear primary beats five vague ones.
  • Description — short, factual, written for humans. Say what you do and where you work.

NAP consistency

Name, address, and phone should match your website and other listings. Inconsistencies confuse customers and systems. If you are service-area only, set that correctly instead of inventing a public street address you do not use.

Hours, services, and attributes

Keep hours accurate, including holidays. Add services that reflect what you actually offer. Attributes (such as women-led, wheelchair accessible, or online estimates) only help if they are true.

Photos and updates

Real photos of the work, team, and premises outperform stock. Add a few strong images and refresh occasionally. Short posts can highlight offers or seasonal work, but consistency beats volume.

Reviews

Ask satisfied customers for honest reviews. Reply calmly to feedback. Do not buy reviews or run schemes that violate Google’s rules — the risk is not worth it.

Link the website

Point the profile at a page that loads fast and matches the offer. If the site is thin or broken on mobile, fix that alongside the profile. See Do I need a website? if you are still deciding.

Service-area businesses

If you go to the customer, configure service areas correctly. Do not publish a street address you do not use. Keep the website and profile aligned on the regions you actually cover. Over-claiming areas creates bad leads and weak trust.

Website and GBP together

Searchers often open both. If the profile says one set of services and the site says another, you look careless. Update both when offers change. Technical SEO on the site still helps; GBP is not a replacement for clear pages.

What not to do

  • Keyword-stuff the business name.
  • Create duplicate listings for the same location.
  • Buy reviews or offer incentives that break Google’s rules.
  • Ignore Q&A or review replies for months.

Most of the value is consistent accuracy, not clever tricks.

Tracking without obsession

Check insights occasionally for calls, direction requests, and searches. Use them to spot broken hours or weak photos, not to chase vanity spikes. Pair GBP changes with website updates so the story stays consistent.

If you run ads, landing pages should match the promise on the profile. Mismatched offers waste spend and erode trust quickly.

A short maintenance rhythm

  1. Monthly: check hours, phone, and photos.
  2. Weekly or as needed: reply to new reviews.
  3. When services change: update categories and website pages together.

GBP will not fix a weak offer. It will amplify a clear one. If you want setup or cleanup done with a site build, that sits inside website work.

Worldwide clients are fine; the same accuracy rules apply whether customers find you on Maps locally or via search more broadly.

Keep it accurate

Accuracy compounds. A correct profile plus a clear website is usually enough for small operators. Clever tricks are optional; honesty and consistency are not.
