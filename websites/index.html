Websites

Small business websites, built to be found.

Hand-built sites for businesses that need clear pages, fast load times, and a sensible path into Google — without a page-builder mess.

Start a project Do I need a website?

01 / What you get

A site that answers the questions people already have.

Most small businesses do not need a complex platform. They need a place that loads quickly on a phone, states what they do, shows proof, and makes contact easy. That is the brief here.

Work is scoped in writing before anything is built. You see progress during the project. When it is done, you own the domain, the code, and the accounts. Nothing is locked to a proprietary builder.

Design & build

One page or multi-page

Layouts that fit the business: services, hours, menus, pricing outlines, photos, and a clear call to action. Hand-coded HTML and CSS. No page-builder bloat.

Search

Technical SEO that ships with the site

Clean URLs, titles, descriptions, headings, sitemap, robots, and schema where it helps. Built so search engines can read the pages without guesswork.

Local presence

Google Business Profile

Setup or cleanup of your listing so name, categories, hours, photos, and website match. Useful when people search on Maps or “near me”.

Ops

Domain, hosting, email

Help choosing and connecting the pieces under your ownership. DNS, hosting, and business email set up so you are not guessing later.

02 / How it works

Four steps. Fixed scope.

01

Brief

You send what you need: services, audience, examples you like, and any hard constraints. No charge for a clear no.

02

Scope

You get a written scope and price before work starts. Pages, features, and timeline are explicit. Changes are discussed, not assumed.

03

Build

You see drafts as they land. Feedback is collected in short rounds so the site does not stall in endless revisions.

04

Handover

Launch, checklist, and ownership. Domain, hosting, and repository stay in your name. Optional follow-up for small edits.

03 / Who this fits

Built for operators, not committees.

This work suits trades, hospitality, tutoring, clinics, and other small teams who sell a clear offer. If you already know what you do and need the web to stop leaking enquiries, it is a fit.

It is less of a fit if you want a massive ecommerce catalogue, a custom app, or a six-month brand programme. Those projects need different teams. You will be told early if that is the case.

Search and Google Business Profile

A good site helps, but it is not the whole picture. Categories, NAP consistency, photos, and reviews on Google Business Profile matter for local discovery. Those pieces can be set up or cleaned up with the site work. See Google Business Profile basics for the practical checklist.

What you own

You own the domain registration, hosting account, and code. There is no monthly “platform tax” required to keep the site online beyond ordinary hosting. If you want ongoing help later, that is optional and month to month.

What “hand-built” means here

Pages are written in HTML and CSS (and light JavaScript where needed). There is no drag-and-drop theme stack sitting underneath. That keeps the site small, predictable, and easier to host. It also means changes are deliberate: you are not fighting a page builder’s defaults every time you want a layout tweak.

Copy can be written with you or from your notes. Photos can be yours or a short shot list. The aim is a site that sounds like the business, not like a template filled with placeholders.

SEO without stuffing

Technical SEO is the boring, useful layer: one clear title and description per page, sensible headings, internal links, a sitemap, and markup that helps search engines understand the page type. Content is written for people first. Keyword lists are not pasted into paragraphs.

Local visibility often depends as much on Google Business Profile as on the site. Categories, hours, photos, and reviews should match. If those are messy, cleaning them up is part of the same job as shipping the site.

Timelines and feedback

Most small sites move quickly once the brief is clear. The slow parts are usually missing copy, undecided services, or waiting on photos. Feedback is collected in short rounds so the project does not stall in endless “one more tweak” loops. If something is out of scope, it is priced and scheduled separately instead of quietly expanding.

After launch

You can leave the site alone, ask for small edits later, or plan a second phase (extra pages, booking, or a simple workflow). Nothing requires a long retainer. Hosting costs stay ordinary. If you later want AI help for quoting or follow-ups, that is a separate scoped piece of work — see the AI page.
